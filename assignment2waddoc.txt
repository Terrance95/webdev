    <h3>HTML Listing</h3>
    
    <p>These tags are used to build lists in HTML. They organize related items into either bulleted or numbered structures, which makes content highly readable and accessible.</p>
    
    <ul>
        <li>Unordered List (ul): Creates a bulleted list.</li>
        <li>Ordered List (ol): Creates a numbered list.</li>
        <li>List Item (li): Represents an individual item within a list.</li>
    </ul>

    <ol>
        <li>First item in the ordered list</li>

    </ol>

    <p>Modern tags / Equivalents (Semantic Wrappers) for ul, ol, li are semantic layout containers that you must wrap around your lists to give them structural meaning to search engines and screen readers.</p>
    
    <p>These semantic tags are:</p>
    
    <ol>
        <li>nav: Represents a section of navigation links.</li>
        <li>section: Defines a thematic grouping of content, often with a heading.</li>
        <li>article: Represents a self-contained piece of content that could be distributed independently.</li>
        <li>aside: Contains content that is tangentially related to the main content, like sidebars or callouts.</li>
        <li>header: Represents introductory content or navigational aids for its nearest ancestor sectioning content.</li>
        <li>footer: Represents the footer for its nearest ancestor sectioning content, often containing metadata or related links.</li>
    </ol>

    <p>If your list consists of website links to navigate your site, wrap it in a &lt;nav&gt; block.</p>
